Nine countries, one payroll tool: what "regional reach" actually means

Most payroll software is built for one country and expanded outward, market by market, each addition bolted onto assumptions the original design never planned for. Mikaju started from the opposite premise: East and Central Africa is one region with nine different statutory regimes, and a business operating across borders shouldn't need nine different tools to run its people.

Today Mikaju covers Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, Ethiopia, Burundi, South Sudan, Somalia, and DR Congo from a single installation. A company with a head office in Nairobi and a branch in Kampala doesn't juggle two payroll systems — it selects the country at setup for each entity, and the correct PAYE bands, social security rates, health insurance, and levies load automatically for each one. Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Rwanda, and Ethiopia currently run on our full statutory engine, with tax certificates, complete social security modeling, and every locally required field captured out of the box. Burundi, South Sudan, Somalia, and DR Congo run on our core engine today — income tax and primary social contributions handled correctly, with deeper statutory detail rolling out on the same cadence documented on Tax Updates.

Regional reach isn't just a country list, though. It's the offline-first architecture that makes the tool usable in a branch office with patchy connectivity in Juba as reliably as in a fibre-connected HQ in Nairobi. It's a tax engine designed from day one as versioned, swappable rate tables per country, so adding statutory depth for Ethiopia doesn't mean re-architecting what already works for Kenya. And it's one subscription, one price in USD across all nine countries, so growing into a new market doesn't come with a new vendor negotiation.

The nine-country footprint reflects where East and Central African businesses actually operate — often across two or three of these borders at once, managing a workforce that a single-country tool was never built to serve. That's the gap Mikaju exists to close, and the country list keeps growing in depth, not just breadth. If you're running payroll across more than one of these countries and want to talk about what full coverage would look like for your setup, reach out at enterprise@mikajupayroll.com.

25 August 2026 · Product

Why we built Mikaju offline-first

Payroll cannot wait for a stable internet connection. Across the 9 countries Mikaju supports, connectivity is inconsistent in exactly the moments payroll matters most — month-end, in branch offices, during fieldwork. We built Mikaju to run every core function entirely on-device, syncing to the cloud only when a connection is available, so a bad connection on payday is never a blocker.

25 August 2026 · Compliance

One tax engine, nine countries

Most payroll tools are built for a single country's statutory rules and bolt on others later. Mikaju's tax engine was designed from the start as a set of versioned, per-country rate tables — PAYE bands, social security, health insurance, and levies — so adding statutory depth for a new country doesn't require rebuilding the underlying engine. It's also why rate changes ship as data updates, not app rewrites: see Tax Updates for the log.
